    What Can a Junk Removal Company Take? A Simple Guide for Cleveland

    2 min read·By Cleveland Junk Removal Pros

    Furniture, appliances, electronics, yard waste and more. Here is what we haul away in Cleveland, and what happens to it after it leaves.

    Key takeaways

    • We take furniture, appliances, electronics, yard waste, full cleanouts and demo debris.
    • Tell us about unusual or hazardous items before pickup so they are handled safely.
    • Usable items are donated, recyclables are recycled, and the rest is disposed of properly.

    Looking at a pile of things you don’t want anymore? The first question is simple: can a junk removal team take all of this? In most cases, yes. Here is a plain guide to what we haul away for homes and businesses in Cleveland, and what happens to it after it leaves.

    Furniture

    Couches, mattresses, dressers, tables and chairs are some of the most common things we remove. Big pieces are heavy, hard to carry down stairs and too large for a regular trash can. We can pick them up from the curb, or carry them out of your home or office for you.

    Appliances

    Old refrigerators, stoves, washers and dryers are heavy and awkward to move. We remove them safely and follow eco-friendly practices, so harmful materials stay out of landfills. Read more in our guide to getting rid of appliances and electronics the right way.

    Electronics (e-waste)

    Outdated TVs, computers and office equipment should not go in the trash. We make sure electronics are recycled or disposed of according to local environmental regulations.

    Yard and landscaping waste

    • Branches, sod, dirt and mulch
    • Leaves, brush and other green waste
    • Yard debris after a landscaping project or a storm

    Whole rooms, homes and buildings

    Sometimes it is not one item but a whole space. We clear entire homes, apartments and commercial spaces, from the attic to the basement. See our property cleanout services.

    Demolition debris and unusual items

    From interior gut-outs to full structure removal, we also clean up the debris that comes with demolition. If you have something unusual, oversized or hazardous, tell us about it when you call. Some items need extra care, and we will explain how they will be handled safely.

    Where does your junk go?

    We try to keep as much as possible out of the landfill. Usable items are donated to one of the many local organizations we work with. Recyclables are recycled. Everything else is disposed of in line with local and state rules.

    Not sure about an item?

    Just ask. Call us at (216) 301-8599, or send photos through our contact form, and we will give you a free, no-obligation quote.

    How to Get Ready for Your Junk Removal Pickup: 7 Simple Steps

    2 min read·By Cleveland Junk Removal Pros

    A little planning makes pickup day faster and easier. Follow these seven simple steps before our truck arrives.

    Key takeaways

    • Make a list and send photos so your quote is accurate.
    • Mark what stays, and check drawers and pockets before pickup.
    • Clear a path and tell us about stairs, basements or very heavy items.

    A little planning makes junk removal day faster, easier and less stressful. Here are seven simple steps to follow before our truck arrives.

    1. Walk through and make a list

    Go room by room and write down what needs to go: furniture, appliances, boxes, yard waste and so on. A list helps you see the size of the job and makes sure nothing is forgotten.

    2. Take photos and send them to us

    Photos help us understand the job and give you a more accurate quote. Our contact form lets you upload pictures of your project, or you can describe it when you call.

    3. Mark what stays

    Put a sticky note or a piece of tape on anything you are keeping, or move it to one room. Clear labels mean nothing important gets mixed up with the junk.

    4. Check drawers, pockets and boxes

    Before furniture leaves, open every drawer and look inside boxes, bags and coat pockets. People often find old papers, photos, cash or jewelry they forgot about.

    5. Clear a path

    Make sure hallways, stairs and doorways are easy to walk through. If you can, leave space in the driveway or in front of your home for the truck.

    6. Tell us about tricky spots

    Let us know about narrow stairs, basements, attics or very heavy items ahead of time. It helps our team bring the right tools and plan the job.

    7. Think about donation

    Many things you no longer need can still help someone else. Usable items are donated to one of the many local organizations we work with, so good things don’t end up in a landfill.

    Old Appliances and Electronics: How to Get Rid of Them the Right Way

    2 min read·By Cleveland Junk Removal Pros

    Fridges, stoves, TVs and computers need more care than a regular trash bag. Here is why, and what to do before they go.

    Key takeaways

    • Fridges, freezers and AC units hold refrigerant that must be handled properly.
    • Older tube TVs and monitors can contain lead, so they should not go in the trash.
    • Wipe or remove your data from computers and phones before they leave.

    Old appliances and electronics are some of the trickiest things to get rid of. They are heavy, and many of them contain materials that should not end up in a landfill. Here is what to know.

    Why appliances need special care

    Refrigerators, freezers and air conditioners contain refrigerant. Under federal rules, refrigerant has to be removed safely before these appliances are scrapped, so it does not leak into the air. Washers, dryers and stoves are mostly metal that can often be recycled.

    Why electronics don’t belong in the trash

    TVs, computers, monitors and office equipment are known as e-waste. Older tube TVs and monitors can contain lead, and many electronics hold metals that can be recovered and reused. That is why e-waste should be recycled or disposed of properly.

    Before they go: a quick checklist

    • Empty the fridge or freezer and clean out any food.
    • Unplug and disconnect washers and dryers if you can do it safely, or ask us first.
    • Back up and wipe your data. Sign out of accounts and reset computers, tablets and phones, or remove the hard drive.
    • Gather cords and remotes so everything goes together.

    How we handle it

    We safely remove and dispose of refrigerators, stoves, washers, dryers and more, following eco-friendly practices to keep harmful materials out of landfills. Electronics are recycled or disposed of according to local environmental regulations. See our junk removal and disposal services.

    Something unusual or hazardous?

    Some items need extra care. Our specialized disposal services are designed for unusual, oversized or hazardous materials. Tell us what you have when you call, and we will explain how it will be handled.

    • Hoarding disorder is a recognized mental health condition, so lead with kindness.
    • Agree on what stays before anything is removed.
    • Our team handles hoarder cleanouts with compassion, care and confidentiality.

    If someone you love is living in a hoarding situation, you may feel worried, frustrated or unsure where to start. That is normal. With patience and the right help, things can get better.

    It is more than a messy house

    Hoarding disorder is a recognized mental health condition. People who hoard often feel real distress at the idea of throwing things away. Understanding this helps you lead with kindness instead of blame.

    Start with a calm conversation

    Choose a quiet moment. Talk about safety and health, not about how the home looks. Try not to throw things away behind the person’s back, because it can break trust and make the next step harder.

    Put safety first

    • Make sure doorways, halls and stairs are clear so people can get out quickly.
    • Watch for fire risks near heaters, stoves and outlets.
    • Look out for mold, pests or spoiled food, which can affect health.

    Get the right support

    A doctor or a mental health professional can help the person with the feelings behind hoarding. A cleanout clears the space, and ongoing support helps keep it that way.

    Agree on a plan

    Before the cleanout, agree together on what stays, what will be donated and what goes. Having a plan gives the person a sense of control.

    How our team helps

    We approach hoarder situations with compassion, care and confidentiality. Our team is trained to handle even the most complex cleanouts with dignity and respect.
